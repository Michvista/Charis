# Body and Fit

Fit is the most important element of any outfit. A well-fitted wardrobe reads expensive; a poor fit reads sloppy regardless of the price of the garment.

## Fit Fundamentals

- Shoulders: the seam should sit exactly at the shoulder bone, not droop or dig in.
- Sleeves: end at the wrist bone; a shirt cuff should peek slightly past a jacket sleeve.
- Trousers: break once or twice at the shoe; no pooling or tightness.
- Tops: no pulling at the buttons, and the fabric should hang without bunching.

## Silhouette and Body Shape

- Highlight the waist with tapered or high-waisted cuts to create a clean line.
- Balance proportions: if the top is loose, keep the bottom fitted, and vice versa.
- Vertical lines (long jackets, monochrome colors, straight cuts) lengthen and streamline.

## Tailoring

- Off-the-rack rarely fits perfectly; hemming trousers and adjusting jacket sleeves are cheap and transform the look.
- A blazer or suit that fits the shoulders is worth buying even if the rest needs tailoring.
- Formal occasions are where fit matters most — a well-tailored look elevates everything.

## Comfort and Movement

- An outfit must be wearable: you should be able to sit, move, and walk comfortably.
- If a garment is so tight it restricts movement, it is the wrong size, not the wrong style.
- Choose appropriate ease for the activity — more room for active settings, closer cuts for formal.

## Fit vs Formality

- Casual can tolerate a relaxed fit, but never a sloppy one.
- Formal requires the closest, sharpest fit.
- The same garment in the right fit can pass for a more formal setting than its fabric would suggest.
