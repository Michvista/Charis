# Color Theory

Color coordination makes an outfit feel intentional. These are principles, not hard numerical rules — use them as guidance alongside the outfit's formality and fabric.

## Neutrals as the Foundation

- Navy, grey, charcoal, black, beige, cream, and white anchor most outfits.
- Build a base with neutrals, then add one accent color for interest.
- When unsure, choose tonal combinations from the same color family.

## Complementary and Analogous Pairings

- Complementary colors (opposites on the wheel, e.g. navy and tan, burgundy and forest) create contrast and balance.
- Analogous colors (neighbors, e.g. olive, khaki, tan) create a calm, monochromatic feel.
- Limit an outfit to 2–3 main colors plus a neutral anchor.

## Pattern Coordination

- When a bold pattern is present, keep the rest of the outfit solid or tonal.
- Do not mix two loud patterns unless they share a base color; stripes + checks can work if one is very subtle.
- Scale matters: large patterns pair with smaller, quieter ones.

## Warm vs Cool Tones

- Warm-toned garments (beige, camel, rust, olive) pair naturally with other warm tones and warm neutrals.
- Cool tones (navy, grey, burgundy, forest) pair with cool neutrals.
- Mixing warm and cool anchors (e.g. camel coat over navy) works when the base pieces are neutral.

## Dressing Up and Down

- Darker, lower-contrast combinations read more formal; high-contrast or bright colors read casual or bold.
- For formal occasions, favor muted, low-saturation colors over bright or neon hues.
- For casual occasions, color can be playful, but keep one grounding neutral in the mix.
