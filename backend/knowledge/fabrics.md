# Fabrics

Fabric choice drives how an outfit reads (formal vs casual) and how it performs (warmth, breathability, drape). Always match fabric weight and texture to the season and occasion.

## Linen

- Highly breathable and lightweight; ideal for warm weather, summer, and beach events.
- Wrinkles easily — acceptable for relaxed settings, avoid for crisp formal occasions.
- Pairs well with cotton and loose tailoring. Great for shirts, suits, and trousers in hot climates.

## Cotton

- Versatile and breathable; the foundation of casual and smart-casual wardrobes.
- Oxford and poplin shirts work for office; jersey and chambray skew casual.
- Denim is cotton — casual by default, elevated only with structured styling.

## Wool

- Warm, structured, and drapes well; the default fabric for tailoring and formal wear.
- Worsted wool suits are formal; merino knits are smart-casual; heavy tweeds are cold-weather casual.
- Keep wool away from warm-weather events unless it is a very light tropical wool.

## Silk

- Luxurious, smooth, and slightly dressy; suits evening and formal occasions.
- Use for ties, scarves, shirts, and dresses when a polished sheen is desired.
- Delicate — not for rugged or outdoor settings.

## Denim

- Casual by nature; reads informal even when dark and well fitted.
- Dark rinse denim can pass for smart-casual with a blazer; light or distressed denim stays casual.

## Breathability and Season

- Warm months: linen, cotton, seersucker, and light blends.
- Cool months: wool, cashmere, flannel, and heavier knits.
- Synthetic-heavy fabrics trap heat and look less refined; prefer natural fibers for both comfort and polish.

## Texture Coordination

- Pair smooth fabrics with smooth (suit + silk tie) or contrast deliberately (denim jacket + linen shirt).
- Too many clashing textures in one outfit (leather + wool + denim + fleece) feels messy; cap an outfit at two or three distinct textures.
