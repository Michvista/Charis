# Occasion Styling

Style advice must start from the occasion: what is happening, where, at what time of day, and at what formality. The same garment can be right or wrong purely based on the setting.

## Evening Dinners and Galas

- Formal dinners need a dark, tailored silhouette — a suit or dress with structured lines.
- Closed-toe polished shoes are expected.
- Keep accessories minimal and refined; a single statement piece at most.

## Daytime / Garden Events

- Lighter colors, breathable fabrics, and softer tailoring.
- Flat or low-heeled footwear that works on grass.
- Hats and sunglasses are appropriate; keep them coordinated with the palette.

## Beach and Outdoor Events

- Lightweight, breathable fabrics are non-negotiable — linen, cotton, seersucker.
- Footwear should be sand-friendly: loafers, espadrilles, or nothing (if the event allows).
- Avoid heavy wool, dark-heavy colors, and formal shine; the setting is relaxed even if the event is dressy.

## Weddings

- Match the dress code announced (black tie, cocktail, casual beach).
- As a guest, avoid white and avoid out-dressing the couple.
- Comfort matters for long ceremonies: choose shoes you can wear all day.

## Business and Office

- Conservative colors and clean silhouettes read as professional.
- When in doubt, choose the more structured option — a blazer or jacket elevates instantly.
- Footwear should be closed and polished.

## Travel and Commuting

- Prioritize wrinkle-resistant fabrics and comfortable shoes.
- Layering solves temperature swings — a jacket or knit that can be removed.
- Choose pieces that work for both the journey and the destination occasion.

## The Golden Rule

- When uncertain, choose the simpler, more neutral, more structured option. It is easier to dress a neutral base up or down than to fix a loud or overly casual one.
