# Outfit Coordination

Coordination is about proportion, layering, and balance — making the pieces work as a single silhouette rather than a pile of garments.

## Proportion

- Balance volume: a loose top pairs with a slim bottom and vice versa.
- Avoid all-over baggy or all-over tight fits; contrast in volume creates a polished look.
- High-waisted bottoms lengthen the leg; cropped tops and jackets work best with longer bottoms.

## Layering

- Layer from light to heavy and thin to thick: tee to shirt to knit to jacket.
- Ensure each layer is slightly visible for depth — a collar, a sleeve, a hem.
- Keep the color story consistent across layers; contrasting an inner layer with an outer layer works when both are neutral.

## Silhouette

- Choose one focal point per outfit: structured shoulders, a statement piece, or a fitted waist.
- Long outer layers over short bottoms, or short jackets over long tops, create balanced vertical lines.
- Match the silhouette's formality to the event — sharp and structured for formal, relaxed but clean for casual.

## Coordination Rules of Thumb

- A full outfit generally includes: top, bottom, shoes, and one layer (jacket/knit) for definition.
- Accessories (belt, watch, bag) should share a metal or leather tone to feel cohesive.
- When two garments compete for attention, remove one.

## Avoiding Common Errors

- Don't combine two identical-purpose loud items (two statement jackets, two bold patterns).
- Don't pair athletic pieces (track pants, trainers) with formal tailoring unless deliberately styled.
- Don't ignore the shoes — footwear anchors the whole outfit and must match the formality level.
